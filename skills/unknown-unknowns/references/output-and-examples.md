# Output shape and examples

## Output shape

1. **The gap that matters most.** One or two sentences: what the user may not know, which layer it is from, and what it changes in their plan.
2. **Other gaps,** numbered, most important first. Each one:
   - what you may not know (plain words), and its layer: inside the box or outside it;
   - why it matters here (which part of the plan it changes);
   - evidence (the source, or the user's words it contrasts with);
   - cheapest next move (read this, ask this person, try this small test).
3. **Assumptions to confirm,** numbered, as either-or questions. Each has a recommended answer and one line on what it changes. The first question is the one whose answer can change the others.
4. **Probe and tripwires.** The one small, early contact with reality, with its cost and what it could reveal. Then two to four tripwires: the signal, and what to reconsider when it appears.
5. **Terms you may need,** only if the field is new to the user. Each term with a one-line meaning.
6. **Already open.** One line listing the questions the user named themselves, so they are not lost.
7. **Not checked.** One line on what you could not verify or find.

Keep it short. Leave out any section that has nothing in it, except the probe. The examples below illustrate the shape; their facts stand in for what the roles would return with sources.

## Examples

### First marathon

**Picture.** Goal: run a first marathon. Plan: "a race in three months, my friend already signed us both up; I'll run 10 km every day". Known: can run 10 km. Constraints: a job with frequent travel.

**Box.** Field: running training. Goal: finish the race. People: the user. Time: from now to race day. Premises: daily 10 km is enough training; the race date is fixed.

**Gap that matters most (inside the box).** The plan has no long run. The hard part of a marathon comes after 30 km, and common beginner plans build around one long run a week that grows over the weeks. Daily 10 km never takes the body near that distance.

**Other gaps.**

1. Outside the box, goal wall. The date was set by the friend signing up ("my friend already signed us both up"). If the point is to do it together, a half marathon on the same day meets that end with far less risk. Changes: which race to run, the biggest choice in the plan.
2. Outside the box, field wall. "A job with frequent travel" against a plan that needs a run every day and a long run every weekend. The plan says nothing about hotel weeks. Next move: mark the travel weeks in the calendar first.
3. Inside the box. Common beginner plans run four to five months; three months leaves little room to build up, and building up too fast is a common cause of injury among new runners.
4. Inside the box, from people who did it. First-timers often say they did not expect race-day logistics: a very early start, long queues at the start, chafing over the distance.

**Assumptions to confirm.**

1. Is the point (a) running with your friend, or (b) the marathon distance itself? Recommended: (a), since the date came from your friend's sign-up. Changes: whether gap 1 replaces the whole plan.
2. In travel weeks, can you (a) run near the hotel or in its gym, or (b) not at all? Changes: how many real training weeks you have.

**Probe and tripwires.** Probe: in week four, run slowly for two and a half hours on a weekend. Cost: one morning. It shows whether your body, your fueling, and your weekend schedule hold up at a duration you have never run. Tripwires: the same pain three days in a row (cut the distance before anything else); missing two long runs in a row because of travel (switch to the half marathon).

**Terms you may need.** Long run: the one slow, long session each week. Easy pace: slow enough to talk. Taper: cutting volume in the last weeks so you start rested.

### First book, self-published

**Picture.** Goal: publish a finished novel. Plan: "upload it to Amazon next week, I'll do the cover myself in Canva". Known: the manuscript is edited. Constraints: little money. Open: "should I use a pen name?"

**Gap that matters most (inside the box).** Most self-published books are found through the store's categories, keywords, and the cover as a small thumbnail. "Upload next week" leaves no time for any of the three, and they are hard to fix once reviews and rankings start.

**Other gaps.**

1. Inside the box. Enrolling the ebook in the store's exclusive programme means it cannot be sold elsewhere for that period. Changes: whether to go wide or exclusive, which is the first decision, not a later one. Next move: read the programme terms before uploading.
2. Outside the box, time wall. The plan stops at upload. Authors who did this often report that launch week is the easy part and that sales depend on what they do in the months after. Changes: whether there is time and money for anything after launch.

**Assumptions to confirm.**

1. Is success (a) readers and reviews, or (b) income? Recommended: (a). Changes: price and launch plan.

**Probe and tripwires.** Probe: show the cover, at thumbnail size, next to ten books in your genre to five readers of the genre, and ask which they would click. Cost: an afternoon. Tripwire: if no one picks yours, fix the cover before setting a launch date.

**Already open.** Pen name.

### "Make my video look nicer"

**Picture.** Goal: a travel video that looks better. Plan: "play with the filters". Known: basic cutting.

**Gap that matters most (inside the box).** Colorists split this into two jobs done in order: first correction (make shots accurate and match each other), then grading (give them a look). Filters jump straight to the look, which is why shots from different times of day still clash.

**Terms you may need.** Exposure: overall brightness. White balance: removing a colour cast. Shot matching: making consecutive shots look like the same moment. LUT: a preset colour transform, applied after correction.

**Assumptions to confirm.** Do you want it to look (a) natural, or (b) stylised? Recommended: (a). Offer three short descriptions of looks so the user can pick one instead of describing it.

**Probe.** Correct and match one 30-second sequence before touching the rest, and compare it with the filtered version side by side.

### Nothing to find

**Picture.** Goal: a laptop for programming. Plan: three named models compared on RAM, battery, keyboard, and repair record, with a budget and a return window.

**Finding.** Inside the box, the picture already covers what a reviewer would check. Outside it, nothing passes the tests: the user named who uses it (themself), for how long (until it breaks), and what they will do if it disappoints (return it). Say so and name what was checked. The probe still stands: the return window is the probe. Use the laptop for a full working day in its first week, while it can still go back.
